# peptide-spectrum-matching-scoring

## Summary

MetaMiner's peptidogenomics scoring pipeline: tandem MS spectra are searched against target/decoy databases of putative RiPP precursor peptides using a modified Dereplicator scoring function (with blind modification search), peptide-spectrum match p-values are computed with MS-DPR, and identifications are filtered with a target-decoy FDR estimate (1% threshold, down to 0% for conservative comparisons).

## When to use

Use when scoring peptide-spectrum matches between GNPS tandem MS spectra and a precursor peptide database derived from genomes/metagenomes, and when controlling identifications by target-decoy FDR.

## When NOT to use

- The spectra lack paired genomics/metagenomics data — as noted in the paper, the other 99% of spectra in GNPS cannot be searched against genomes
- The peptides of interest are cyclic and require blind modification searches — MetaMiner does not perform blind modification searches for cyclic peptides due to computational complexity
- The task is generic non-RiPP spectral annotation (e.g. dereplication against known structures with Dereplicator+ or molecular networking alone) rather than peptidogenomics search against a precursor peptide database

## Inputs

- Tandem mass spectrometry spectra from GNPS datasets (e.g. MSV000078839, MSV000078604)
- Target database of putative RiPP precursor peptides from 6-frame translation of genomes/metagenomic contigs
- Decoy database generated by random shuffling of each target precursor peptide

## Outputs

- Scored peptide-spectrum matches with Dereplicator scores
- PSM p-values computed by MS-DPR
- FDR-filtered set of RiPP identifications (target-decoy, e.g. at 0% and 1% FDR thresholds)
- Spectrally enlarged RiPP identification set via spectral networks

## How to apply

Build the target database of putative RiPP precursor peptides (via motif-ORF or all-ORF enumeration of ORFs shorter than the default 200 amino acid threshold), then create a decoy database by randomly shuffling each peptide in the target precursor database. Search spectra against the target and decoy databases using MetaMiner's modified version of Dereplicator, whose dynamic programming approach restricts the number of modifications and penalizes high-score matches with more than one modification (blind modifications are searched for linear peptides, not cyclic ones). Compute peptide-spectrum match p-values with MS-DPR and convert them to FDR estimates via the target-decoy approach, applying a 1% FDR threshold for identification. Finally, enlarge the set of RiPP identifications through spectral networking before counting known versus unknown RiPPs.

## Evaluation signals

- At 0% FDR on the ACTI dataset (MSV000078839/MSV000078604), motif-ORF mode yields 3 unknown + 5 known RiPPs while all-ORF mode yields only 2 known RiPPs
- At 1% FDR, 8 RiPPs total are identified in the ACTI dataset
- In the positive control dataset, MetaMiner correctly identifies all 18 known RiPPs
- Decoy PSM count is used to estimate FDR via the target-decoy approach; p-values come from MS-DPR and must be consistent with the chosen threshold
- Each high-scoring match should carry at most one modification, since the dynamic programming scoring penalizes matches with more than one modification

## Limitations

- Blind modification searches are not performed for cyclic peptides due to inherent computational complexity
- The motif-ORF strategy is not sensitive: peptides without sequence similarity to known RiPPs are missed
- Only 38 RiPPs were identified from 10 million spectra at 1% FDR, and many spectral families belong to non-RiPP compound classes (e.g. non-ribosomal peptides per Dereplicator+ analysis)
- 99% of GNPS spectra lack paired genomics/metagenomics data and cannot be searched
